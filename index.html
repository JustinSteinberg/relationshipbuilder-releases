relationshipBuilder

Friendships don't end from conflict. They end from drift.

relationshipBuilder reads your Messages history on your Mac and tells you, each day, which one to three people are worth reaching out to — the ones you actually care about and haven't spoken to in too long.

It doesn't nag you about everyone. It ranks by regret: who would you be sad to lose touch with? Those people surface first.

Download for Mac

Free. macOS 14 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel · Requires Messages on your Mac

Your messages never leave your Mac

This app reads your text messages, so you deserve a straight answer about where they go: nowhere. All analysis happens locally on your machine. There is no account, no server, no telemetry, and nothing is uploaded.

An optional setting lets you use the Claude API for conversation suggestions. It is off by default, requires you to paste your own API key, and even then the app only ever asks for talking points — it never writes messages for you.

Installing

  1. Open the downloaded relationshipBuilder.dmg.
  2. Drag the app into your Applications folder. Don't run it from the disk image window — permissions won't stick if you do.
  3. Open your Applications folder and double-click relationshipBuilder.
  4. macOS will ask whether you're sure you want to open an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open. This happens once.
  5. The app will walk you through granting Full Disk Access — see below.

Why it asks for Full Disk Access

Apple stores your message history in a protected file that no app can read without your explicit permission. There's no narrower permission available — Full Disk Access is the only switch Apple provides, and macOS requires you to flip it yourself in System Settings rather than through a popup.

The app shows you exactly how, but the short version: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, click +, choose relationshipBuilder from Applications, and make sure the toggle is on. Then quit and reopen the app — macOS only checks this permission at launch.

What it looks at

Message timing and frequency, who reached out last, whether a question of yours went unanswered, and roughly how long it's been. It uses your Contacts to show names instead of phone numbers.

It shows one person at a time, as a card. Not a to-do list of 200 people — that's a guilt machine, not a habit.
