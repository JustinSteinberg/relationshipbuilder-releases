Overview

If you’re reading this,
it’s not too late.

RelationshipBuilder shows you who to text, starting with the people you’d hate to lose.

You know how it happens. A message comes in on a bad week. You want to answer properly, so you wait for a better moment. The better moment never comes. A month goes by, then another, and by then it feels strange to write at all.

Most of the time you can still fix it. Sometimes you can’t. Nobody warns you that a conversation was the last one.

So the app keeps track for you. It brings up one person at a time, the ones you’d miss most first, and reminds you where you left off. Keep going as long as you like. It stops when there’s nobody left to bring up.

Research

This isn’t just a hunch. Two things researchers have found:

We regret what we didn’t do. Mistakes sting at first. But the things we never got around to stay with us longer, because the reason we put them off stops making sense later. Gilovich & Medvec, 1994, replicated 2022
It means more to them than you think. In thirteen studies with almost six thousand people, a message out of the blue mattered more to the person getting it than the sender expected. The longer the quiet, the bigger the surprise, and the more it meant. The gap you feel awkward about is the reason it lands. Liu, Rim, Min & Min, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2022

Which is why the app doesn’t grade your friendships or hand you a to-do list. It just makes it easy to say something while saying it still counts.

What it does

Shows you what’s still waiting You don’t have to scroll back through months of texts. It finds the questions you never answered and the conversations that stopped mid sentence.
Remembers what you talked about What was going on with them, and how long ago. Enough to say something real instead of sorry.
Gives you a nudge, not a script A few things worth asking about. The words are yours.
Uses Messages It works with the Messages app you already have. Write here, and it goes out like any other text.
One person at a time A single card. Answer or skip, and the next one comes up. Two hundred names at once only makes you feel bad, and feeling bad is why people quit.

Privacy

The app reads your texts, so here is the plain answer about where they go. Nowhere. Everything happens on your own Mac. Nothing is uploaded. No account, no server, nothing tracked.

It won’t write your messages for you. There is one setting that asks Claude for better conversation ideas. It stays off unless you turn it on, it needs your own API key, and even then it only asks what to talk about. A message worth sending isn’t one a computer wrote.

Install

  1. Open the file you downloaded, relationshipBuilder.dmg.
  2. Drag the app into your Applications folder. Don’t run it from the window that opens, or it will forget its permissions.
  3. Open it from Applications. Your Mac will ask whether you’re sure about an app from the internet. Click Open. It only asks once.
  4. The app will walk you through letting it read your messages, then ask to use Contacts so you see names instead of numbers.

Permissions

Apple keeps your messages in a protected file, and Full Disk Access is the only key that opens it. There is nothing smaller to ask for. Your Mac also won’t let an app ask in a popup, so you have to switch it on yourself.

The app shows you how. The short version: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, click +, pick RelationshipBuilder from Applications, make sure the switch is on, then quit and open it again. Your Mac only checks when the app starts.
