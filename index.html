Overview

If you’re reading this,
it’s not too late.

Your texts are full of good intentions. A message from your sister you meant to answer. A “we should catch up” from someone you actually miss. A question you saw, planned to reply to, and somehow left sitting there for four weeks.

You didn’t decide to lose touch. Life just kept moving, and the conversation disappeared beneath everything that came after it.

RelationshipBuilder helps you pick those threads back up. It looks through your Messages history, finds the conversations that may be worth returning to, reminds you what you were talking about, and gives you one person at a time.

RelationshipBuilder runs on a Mac.

It reads the Messages history stored on a Mac, which is something Apple doesn’t allow apps to do on a phone. Send yourself the link and open it on your Mac.

What it does

Finds the conversations you left hanging Questions you never answered. Conversations that stopped halfway. Messages you meant to come back to and then lost beneath months of newer ones.
Remembers where you left off See what was going on in someone’s life the last time you talked, what you discussed, and how long it has been. You can continue the conversation instead of starting from zero.

Why it works

A couple of findings from psychology sit behind the idea.

We tend to carry the things we never did. Mistakes can hurt more in the moment, but regrets about inaction often last longer. With time, the reasons we had for not acting become less convincing, while the unanswered question of what might have happened remains. Gilovich & Medvec, 1994, replicated 2022
Reaching out usually means more than we expect. Across thirteen experiments involving nearly six thousand people, researchers found that people consistently underestimated how much someone would appreciate an unexpected message. The effect was especially strong when the message came as more of a surprise. Liu, Rim, Min & Min, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2022

That is the idea behind RelationshipBuilder. We often want to reach out, underestimate what the gesture might mean, and then simply forget.

A computer can’t maintain your relationships for you. But it can remember what you meant to come back to.

Privacy

RelationshipBuilder reads your messages, so the important question is obvious: where do they go?

Nowhere.

Your message history is analyzed locally on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded to a RelationshipBuilder server because there is no RelationshipBuilder server. There is no account, no telemetry, and no message database somewhere else.

RelationshipBuilder also does not write your messages for you. There is an optional setting that can use Claude to generate richer conversation ideas. It is off by default, requires an API key you provide yourself, and is used only to suggest things you might want to talk about.

The words you send remain yours.

RelationshipBuilder is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, allowing macOS to verify that the app has not been modified since it was released.

Install

  1. Open the downloaded relationshipBuilder.dmg.
  2. Drag RelationshipBuilder into your Applications folder. Don’t run it directly from the disk image; macOS permissions may not persist correctly.
  3. Open RelationshipBuilder from Applications. The first time, macOS will ask you to confirm that you want to open an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open.
  4. RelationshipBuilder will walk you through granting access to your message history and Contacts.

Permissions

Apple stores your Messages history in a protected part of macOS. Full Disk Access is currently the permission required to read it. macOS does not provide a narrower permission specifically for Messages history.

Apple also does not allow apps to request Full Disk Access through a normal permission popup, so you need to enable it manually.

RelationshipBuilder walks you through the process. The short version is:

System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access → + → RelationshipBuilder

Make sure the switch is on, then quit and reopen RelationshipBuilder. macOS applies the permission when the app launches.

Contacts access is used so RelationshipBuilder can show names instead of phone numbers.
