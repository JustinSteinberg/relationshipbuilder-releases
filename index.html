Overview

If you’re reading this,
it’s not too late.

relationshipBuilder shows you who to reach out to, beginning with the people whose absence you would feel most, and the messages you never answered.

The pattern is familiar. A message arrives during a demanding week. You intend to reply properly rather than carelessly, so you set it aside. Weeks pass. The intention survives; the reply does not.

Usually this is recoverable. Occasionally it is not. No one announces which exchange will be the last, and what you meant to say remains available only for as long as the conversation does.

relationshipBuilder is a regret minimization tool. It presents one person at a time, ordered by whose loss you would feel most, with the context required to resume a conversation rather than restart one. Continue for as long as you like. It ends when there is genuinely no one left to suggest.

Research

The premise is not merely intuitive. Two findings inform the design.

The regrets that endure concern what we did not do. Errors of commission sting more immediately, but errors of omission outlast them. One reason is that the hesitation which prevented action ceases to seem reasonable in retrospect. Gilovich & Medvec, 1994, replicated 2022
We underestimate what a message is worth to the person receiving it. Across thirteen experiments with nearly six thousand participants, unexpected messages were valued more highly by recipients than senders had predicted, and the effect strengthened the more unexpected the contact. The silence you find embarrassing is the very thing that gives the gesture its weight. Liu, Rim, Min & Min, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2022

The application therefore makes no attempt to rate your relationships or to press you toward everyone at once. It reduces the effort required to do the one thing that demonstrably helps, which is to say something while saying it still matters.

What it does

Surfaces the conversations that need you Rather than searching months of history, you see what was left unresolved. Questions never answered, exchanges that stopped mid thought.
Recalls what you last discussed What was happening in their life, and how long ago that was. Enough to open with something specific instead of an apology.
Offers context, not scripts Subjects worth raising and details worth remembering. The words remain yours.
Sends through Messages It works with the Messages app already on your Mac. A reply you compose here is delivered normally, and looks entirely ordinary at the other end.
One person at a time A single card rather than an inventory. Respond or set it aside, and the next appears. Two hundred neglected friendships presented at once produces guilt, and guilt is why applications of this kind go unopened.

Privacy

This application reads your messages, which entitles you to a precise answer about where they go. Nowhere. Analysis takes place entirely on your own computer. Nothing is transmitted. There is no account, no server, and no telemetry.

It does not compose messages on your behalf. One optional setting uses the Claude API to enrich the context it offers. It is disabled by default, it requires an API key you supply yourself, and even once enabled it requests only subjects for discussion. A message worth sending is not one that something else wrote.

The application is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, which confirms to macOS that it has not been altered since it was built.

Install

  1. Open the downloaded relationshipBuilder.dmg.
  2. Drag the application to your Applications folder. Permissions will not persist if it is run from the disk image.
  3. Open it from Applications. macOS will ask whether you are certain about an application downloaded from the internet. Click Open. This happens once.
  4. The application will guide you through granting Full Disk Access, then request access to Contacts so that names appear in place of phone numbers.

Permissions

Your message history resides in a file that Apple protects, and Full Disk Access is the only permission that reaches it. No narrower option exists. macOS also prevents an application from requesting it directly, so it has to be granted by hand.

The application demonstrates the procedure. In brief: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, click +, select relationshipBuilder from Applications, confirm the toggle is enabled, then quit and reopen it. macOS evaluates this permission only at launch.
