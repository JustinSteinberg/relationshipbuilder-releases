You meant to text back. Every day, relationshipBuilder shows you the handful of people you'd most regret losing touch with, and the messages you left hanging.

It was a busy week. The message scrolled up out of sight, replying moved to later, and later became a month. You still think about them. You just never picked the thread back up.

Usually that's fixable. Sometimes it isn't. Nobody tells you which conversation was the last one, and the thing you always meant to say stays sayable only for as long as the thread stays open.

relationshipBuilder is a regret minimization tool. Every day it shows you one to three people worth reaching out to, with everything you need to continue the conversation instead of starting it over.

What it does

Finds the messages that actually need you Instead of scrolling back through months of threads, you see what was left hanging. Questions you never answered, conversations that stopped halfway.
Reminds you what you were talking about What was going on in their life last time, and how long ago that was. So you can open with something real instead of "hey, sorry for the delay."
Gives you insights, not scripts Talking points and context. What to ask about, what to follow up on. What you actually say is yours.
Sends through Messages It connects to the Messages app you already use. Reply from here and it goes out normally. No copy and paste, no second inbox, nothing for the other person to notice.
One person at a time A card a day, not a backlog. A list of two hundred neglected friends is a guilt machine, and guilt is why people stop opening the app.

Your messages never leave your Mac

This app reads your text messages, so you deserve an unambiguous answer about where they go. Nowhere. Everything is analyzed locally on your own computer. Nothing is sent over the internet. There is no account, no server, and no telemetry.

It does not write messages for you. One optional setting uses the Claude API for richer talking points. It is off by default, it needs an API key you provide yourself, and even switched on it only ever asks for things to talk about. A message worth sending isn't one that something else wrote.

Installing

  1. Open the downloaded relationshipBuilder.dmg.
  2. Drag the app to your Applications folder. Don't run it from the disk image window, or permissions won't stick.
  3. Open it from Applications. macOS asks whether you're sure about an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open. This happens once.
  4. The app walks you through granting Full Disk Access, then asks to use Contacts so it can show names instead of phone numbers.

Why it needs Full Disk Access

Your message history lives in a file Apple protects, and Full Disk Access is the only permission that reaches it. There is no narrower option to ask for. macOS also won't let an app request it in a popup, so you have to turn it on yourself.

The app shows you how. Briefly: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, click +, pick relationshipBuilder from Applications, confirm the toggle is on, then quit and reopen it. macOS only checks at launch.

It's not too late yet

Nobody drifts on purpose. It happens in the gaps between busy weeks, and it stays reversible right up until it isn't. The openings are finite. This helps you use them while they're still there.
